How to make a website for your clinic or doctor practice.

⏱ 5 min read 📅 Updated June 2026 ✍ By Neweb team

Patients search "doctor near me" and "best dermatologist in [city]" every minute on Google. A professional clinic website builds trust before the first appointment, handles routine appointment requests without your staff, and keeps your practice discoverable 24/7. Here is how to build one right.

01

Choose the right domain for your clinic

Your domain should be easy to say aloud and easy to spell from memory. Options follow a predictable pattern: drsharmaortho.in, apolloskinmumbai.com, or primecareclinic.in. For a multi-doctor practice, use the clinic brand name rather than an individual doctor's name — it is more future-proof if your team changes.

For healthcare specifically, the .in TLD performs well for local searches in India because Google treats it as a geographic signal. A .com is equally valid and has higher recall in word-of-mouth recommendations. Avoid .health or .clinic extensions — they are newer, cost more, and patients are not yet accustomed to them.

  • Neweb includes your domain free every year — no renewal surprises or domain transfer hassles
  • Avoid registering in someone else's name — the domain owner is the legal owner; it should be the clinic's
02

Create a detailed doctor profile page

The doctor profile is the most trust-building page on a clinic website. Patients arrive at your site after a health concern — they are evaluating whether you are credible, qualified, and the right fit. A thorough profile page converts browsers into booked appointments more effectively than any other content.

For each doctor, include: full name and title, MBBS/MD/MS/DNB degree with institution, year of passing, MCI/NMC registration number (required under NMC Code of Ethics for digital advertising), years of experience, specialisation, clinic timings, languages spoken, and a professional headshot. If the doctor has published research or won awards, list them — patients genuinely notice.

  • The MCI/NMC registration number is not optional for online medical advertising — it is required under Indian regulations
  • A professional headshot in a white coat significantly outperforms casual photos in conversion tests
  • If you have multiple doctors, create individual profile pages for each — this improves SEO for their names
  • Include the speciality in the page title and URL: /dr-meena-sharma-dermatologist-pune
03

Add an appointment booking form or WhatsApp integration

An appointment form captures enquiries when your receptionist is busy or the clinic is closed. Keep it short: name, phone number, preferred date, type of consultation (new/follow-up), and a text field for the chief complaint. Longer forms get abandoned. The submission should trigger an instant WhatsApp confirmation to the patient and an email or WhatsApp notification to your clinic.

Many clinics also link to Practo, 1mg Consult, or Apollo 247 for online booking — this is good practice as these platforms have their own patient discovery traffic. Link to your Practo profile from your website booking section with a clear "Book via Practo" button so patients who prefer that flow are not left searching.

  • Neweb's WhatsApp form integration sends appointment requests directly to your clinic WhatsApp number
  • Add a "Book Emergency Appointment" CTA that calls your direct number — emergencies need a phone, not a form
  • Show consultation fees clearly — it reduces phone calls asking "how much is the consultation?"
  • If you offer video consultations, make that a separate booking option — telemedicine demand remains high post-COVID
04

Ensure compliance with the DPDP Act and medical advertising rules

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDP Act) applies to any website that collects personal data from Indian residents — including patient names, phone numbers, health complaints submitted through contact forms, and email addresses. Healthcare data is classified as sensitive personal data and carries additional obligations.

At minimum, your clinic website must have: a Privacy Policy explaining what data you collect and why, a cookie consent notice for any analytics tools, a Data Processing Notice on appointment forms, and a mechanism for patients to request deletion of their data. Neweb generates a DPDP-compliant privacy policy as part of the clinic template setup.

  • Do not use third-party marketing pixels (Meta Pixel, Google Ads) without explicit patient consent — this can constitute a breach under DPDP
  • Under the Indian Medical Council Regulations, medical advertising must not make comparative claims, guarantee cure, or use patient names/photos without written consent
  • Store patient contact form submissions securely — avoid using free form services that store data in unencrypted third-party databases
  • Review your privacy policy annually or whenever you change data collection practices
05

Set up Google Business for healthcare

Google Business Profile for healthcare has specific category options: "Medical Clinic," "Doctor," "Dermatologist," "Cardiologist," "Pediatrician," and many more. Choosing the right primary category is the single most important ranking decision for local medical search. A general practitioner should use "Doctor" or "General Practitioner" — not "Hospital."

Add each doctor by name in the "Staff" or "People" section of your GBP (available in the Google Maps app under your listing). Add your appointment URL from Practo or your website directly to the "Book" button on your listing. Set your healthcare attributes: insurance accepted, wheelchair accessible, parking availability, languages spoken by staff. If you have not claimed your listing yet, follow our walkthrough on how to get a Google Business listing.

  • Patients searching "cardiologist near me" see the top 3 GBP results — the "Local Pack" — before any organic results
  • Respond to every review mentioning a negative experience with empathy but no clinical details — patient privacy applies even in public replies
  • Post health awareness content (e.g., "World Diabetes Day — free blood sugar screening this Saturday") as Google Posts — they appear prominently in your listing
  • Do not add staff photos to GBP that contain other patients — photo privacy is a compliance issue
06

Collect patient testimonials ethically

Patient testimonials are powerful trust signals but must be collected and displayed within the boundaries of Indian medical advertising regulations. The core rule: you cannot use a patient testimonial that makes a clinical claim ("Dr. Sharma cured my arthritis completely") because that implies a guaranteed outcome. General satisfaction statements ("The staff were very professional and caring") are acceptable.

For Google reviews, there are no such restrictions — Google's platform handles disclosure automatically. Encourage satisfied patients to leave Google reviews via a printed QR code in your waiting room, a WhatsApp message after the appointment, or a card handed with the prescription. Reviews mentioning the doctor's name and specialty ("Dr. Kapoor explained my diagnosis clearly") are extremely valuable for local SEO.

  • Always get written consent before using a patient's name or photo on your website
  • Never publish testimonials with medical claims like "I was cured" or "no side effects" — these violate IMC regulations
  • A printed A5 card with a QR code and "We'd love your feedback — scan to leave a Google review" converts well in waiting rooms
  • Aim for 10+ Google reviews in your first 3 months — this is the threshold that unlocks the star rating display in Maps results

FAQ

Common questions.

Does a clinic website need DPDP compliance in India?

Yes, a clinic website needs DPDP compliance. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 applies to any entity that collects personal data from Indian residents, which includes patient names, phone numbers, email addresses, and any health details submitted through appointment or contact forms. Health information is treated as sensitive, so the obligations are stricter. At minimum your site needs a clear privacy policy explaining what you collect and why, a data processing notice on every form, a consent mechanism, and a way for patients to request deletion of their data. Avoid placing marketing pixels such as Meta Pixel or Google Ads tags without explicit consent, and store form submissions securely rather than in free unencrypted form services. Neweb generates a DPDP-ready privacy policy as part of the clinic template. Review it yearly or whenever your data practices change. A tip: keep public review replies free of any clinical detail to protect patient privacy.

Can I show patient testimonials on my clinic website?

Yes, you can show patient testimonials, but within the limits of Indian medical advertising rules. Testimonials must be general satisfaction statements such as "the staff were caring and the clinic was clean", not clinical claims like "Dr Sharma cured my arthritis", because anything implying a guaranteed outcome can breach the National Medical Commission (Professional Conduct) Regulations. Avoid before and after medical photos and never promise results. Always obtain written consent before publishing a patient's name, photo, or words, and keep that consent on file. Google reviews are different: the platform handles disclosure, so encourage happy patients to leave them using a QR code in your waiting room or a WhatsApp message after the visit. Reviews that name the doctor and speciality, like "Dr Kapoor explained my diagnosis clearly", are excellent for local SEO. A tip: aim for ten or more Google reviews in your first three months to unlock the star rating in Maps.

How should I list my qualifications on a clinic website?

List your full qualifications clearly: your MBBS or BDS degree, the institution, the year of passing, and your MCI or NMC registration number, which is required under the NMC Code of Ethics for digital medical advertising in India. For specialists, add your MD, MS, or DNB specialisation, and any super-specialisation such as DM or MCh. Include years of experience, languages spoken, clinic timings, and a professional headshot, ideally in a white coat, because that consistently outperforms casual photos in patient trust. If you have published research or won awards, mention them, since patients genuinely notice credentials. For a multi-doctor practice, give each doctor their own profile page with the speciality in the URL, for example /dr-meena-sharma-dermatologist-pune, which helps you rank for their name. A tip: state consultation fees on the page to cut routine phone calls and set patient expectations before they book.

Should I use Practo or my own website?

Use both, because they play different roles. Practo, 1mg Consult, and Apollo 247 have their own patient discovery traffic and are good for getting found, but they take a cut and own the patient relationship, so you cannot build loyalty or a brand on them alone. Your own website ranks on Google for your name and speciality, builds long-term trust through detailed doctor profiles, and lets you collect direct, commission-free bookings via a short form or WhatsApp button. The smart approach is to link from your website to your Practo profile for patients who prefer that flow, while making your own appointment form and WhatsApp the primary path. Add an emergency call button for urgent cases, and keep consultation fees visible. Pair this with a verified Google Business Profile so "doctor near me" searches surface your clinic in the local pack. See our guide on ranking on Google Maps in India for more.
