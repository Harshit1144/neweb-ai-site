01
Real estate playbook · 01

Buyers shortlist before they call. Your website is where they decide.

A property buyer or tenant in 2026 behaves the way a traveller books a hotel — visually, on a phone, comparing three or four options before reaching out to anyone. A search for "2BHK in Wakad" or "office space for rent in Andheri" surfaces portals, Google Maps, and individual broker sites. The buyer glances at the photos, the price, the carpet area, and whether a RERA number is shown, and then decides whom to message. For a property dealer or broker, the website is where that decision gets confirmed — its job is to show real listings with honest prices and one clear way to enquire before the buyer's attention moves on.

What changes for property businesses on Neweb is that the website stops being a static brochure that goes stale the moment a flat is sold. It is one source of truth. Mark a listing "sold" or "under offer" and it updates on the listing page, the locality page, and Google Business at once. Upload new project photos from a site visit and they land in the right gallery with the right crop. Buyers read that freshness and accuracy as trust — and trust is what makes them enquire with you instead of the next dealer in the search results.

Real estate playbook · 02

Lead capture should feel like sending a text

The single biggest reason a buyer who landed on your listing does not become an enquiry is friction. Long contact forms. Required login. Pop-ups asking for an email before they have seen the floor plan. Calling a number during office hours when they are browsing at 11pm. Neweb defaults to a different pattern: a short enquiry form (name, phone, budget, locality) and a WhatsApp deep-link with a pre-filled message such as "Hi, I'm interested in the 3BHK in Baner, ref BNR-204. Is it still available?" The enquiry arrives in your own WhatsApp as a structured, qualified lead with the property reference attached. You reply, share more photos, and schedule a site visit — without paying a portal a per-lead fee or handing over your buyer database.

The WhatsApp pattern works disproportionately well for Indian real estate. Buyers, tenants, NRIs and investors all message rather than fill forms, and they expect a quick reply with the floor plan and the next available visit slot. For dealers running many active listings, Neweb tags each lead with its property reference automatically, so you always know which flat the buyer is asking about and can follow up without losing the thread.

Real estate playbook · 03

RERA display and trust signals are non-negotiable

Since the RERA Act came into force, every agent and project advertisement is expected to carry the relevant RERA registration number, and buyers increasingly check for it before they enquire. A property website that hides or omits this looks risky; one that shows the agent RERA number, the project RERA number, and a link to the state RERA portal (MahaRERA, UP-RERA, K-RERA, Gujarat RERA and so on) signals that you operate above board. Neweb gives every listing and project page a dedicated RERA field, prints the number in the footer and on adverts, and adds the standard disclaimer block — so compliance is built in, not bolted on later.

Trust extends beyond RERA. Google reviews from past buyers and tenants, a clear "About the agency" page, real office photos, and team profiles with phone numbers all reduce the buyer's hesitation. Neweb makes the review loop automatic with a WhatsApp follow-up after a closed deal, and adds RealEstateAgent schema so Google can show your star rating and tap-to-call button. The combination — RERA on display, fresh reviews, real photos — is what converts a cautious property search into a confident enquiry.

04
Real estate playbook · 04

Buyer, seller and rental journeys are different — give each its own page

A property business serves at least three distinct audiences, and lumping them onto one page loses all of them. Buyers want filtered listings, locality insight, price trends and virtual tours. Sellers and landlords want a simple "List your property" form, a valuation enquiry, and proof that you market aggressively. Tenants want rental flats and PG options with rent, deposit and furnishing clearly stated. Neweb ships a dedicated section for each — a Buy section with locality and project pages, a Sell/Rent-out lead form, and a Rentals section — each with its own meta title, schema, and call to action, so a search for "sell my flat in Kothrud" or "rental flat in HSR Layout" lands the visitor on exactly the right page.

The locality and project pages are the quiet workhorses. A page for "Property in Hinjewadi" or "Flats in Gota, Ahmedabad" that explains the area — connectivity, schools, price band, upcoming projects — and lists your active inventory there will out-rank a generic homepage for that search every time. Add a virtual tour link, an embedded map, and an enquiry form, and the page does the qualifying for you before the buyer ever picks up the phone.

For the owner, the value is that no part of the online presence is left to chance or to a freelancer who disappears mid-project. Buyers, sellers, tenants and investors can find you, see real listings, verify your RERA number, read your reviews, and enquire — in the time it takes them to send a WhatsApp message.
